NAD+ MRS Pipeline Optimization Summary
=======================================

This file now records TWO optimization campaigns on two different drivers.
Read section 6 first if you are working on the brain downfield fit -- it is the
current one, and it OVERTURNS parts of the April record below (lineshape kernel,
Trp exclusion).  The April sections are kept because they are still authoritative
for processRepeatability.m, a different driver / cohort / metric.

  Sections 1-5 + order note : Apr 2026, processRepeatability.m
                              (scan-rescan REPEATABILITY of NAD self-ratio)
  Section 6                 : Jul 2026, processU01_Brain_1H.m
                              (between-subject CV + NAD self-consistency vs
                              time-domain HSVD "SpecTickle")

Do not compare CV numbers across the two campaigns: April's ~3% is a
same-subject scan-rescan CV; July's ~11-28% is between-DISTINCT-subject

Date: 2026-04-14
Scope: processRepeatability.m cohort (7 groups, all 9.7 ppm):
       S004 sinc, S092 sinc, S092 U01, S139 sinc, S145 sinc, S076 U01, S138 U01

1. BLOCK ALIGNMENT (pars.block_align)
-------------------------------------
Problem:  Time-domain alignment of averaged blocks was a no-op at block
          size = 32 on this cohort (rPre == rPost to 7 decimals). Larger
          block sizes let the TD fit converge, but TD was still inferior
          to FD optimization restricted to the NAD+ region.

Swept:    method {td, fd}, size {32, 64, 128}, FD ppmRange variants.

Winner:   method='fd', size=64, Nfit=256, ppmRange=[8.5 10].
          - FD alignment uses lsqcurvefit on concatenated real/imag
            spectrum within a user-specified ppm window (new helper:
            utils/fidfreqshift_fd.m).
          - size=64 is optimal: size=32 is too noisy to fit, size=128
            leaves only 2 blocks for Pilot-Sinc scans (256 averages).
          - Restricting to [8.5 10] ppm avoids residual water/lipid
            signals dominating the cost function.

Defaults set in dat2mat_svsNAD.m:
    pars.block_align.method   = 'fd'
    pars.block_align.size     = 64
    pars.block_align.Nfit     = 256
    pars.block_align.ppmRange = [8.5 10]


2. LINESHAPE KERNEL (pars.fit.lineShapeOpt)
-------------------------------------------
*** SUPERSEDED FOR BRAIN (processU01_Brain_1H.m): kernel is now DISABLED.
*** Still enable=true for processRepeatability.m.  See section 6.2. ***

Problem:  FD alignment didn't uniformly improve CV. S139 regressed
          (1.62% under current settings) because the downstream Voigt
          fit reacted badly to small shape perturbations that FD
          alignment introduced. Root cause: kernel too narrow to
          absorb asymmetric peak shape, so shape variance leaked
          into peak-area estimates.

Swept:    enable, asymmetric, nSide in {0,2,3,4,5,6}, maxSide in
          {0.50, 0.75, 1.00}. Coarse + fine (11 conditions total).

Winner:   enable=true, asymmetric=true, nSide=4, maxSide=0.50.
          - nSide=4 is a clear plateau: all four nSide=4 configs
            occupy the top 4 slots regardless of maxSide.
          - nSide<4 underfits shape; nSide>4 starts overfitting.
          - maxSide in [0.5, 1.0] is nearly indifferent at nSide=4
            (delta <= 0.10 pp). 0.50 is marginally best.
          - S139 regression fully resolved (1.62% -> 0.04%).
          - S004 further improved (3.51% -> 2.53%).

Defaults set in utils/curvefitAuto_varproBaseline.m (parseLineShapeOpt)
and processRepeatability.m:
    pars.fit.lineShapeOpt.enable     = true
    pars.fit.lineShapeOpt.nSide      = 4
    pars.fit.lineShapeOpt.asymmetric = true
    pars.fit.lineShapeOpt.maxSide    = 0.50


3. HSVD NUISANCE CLEANING (pars.fit.hsvdClean)
----------------------------------------------
Problem:  Pre-fit HSVD subtraction of out-of-range components was
          actively hurting repeatability on this cohort. Increasing K
          or tightening the preserve range caused catastrophic
          scan-to-scan instability (some groups hit >20% CV).

Swept:    enable {on, off}, K in {30, 60, 100, 150}, preserve range
          in {[8.5 11], [8.7 11], [8.75 11], [8.75 12], [9.0 11]}.
          10 conditions.

Winner:   enable=false. Mean CV dropped 5.92% -> 5.14%.
          - Residual RMS was LOWER (6/7 groups) with HSVD off -
            the cleaning step was distorting the spectrum, not
            removing nuisance.
          - Aggressive cleaning (K=100, K=150) caused H6 CV > 30%
            on S004/S145 - overmodeling absorbed signal whose
            absorption flipped scan-to-scan.
          - Tight preserve range [9.0 11] clipped the H4 region
            (8.8-9.05) -> 26% mean CV.
          - Runner-up [8.75 12] (5.41%) retained HSVD for edge
            cases but was still worse than off.

Defaults set in dat2mat_svsNAD.m and processRepeatability.m:
    pars.fit.hsvdClean.enable = false
    pars.fit.hsvdClean.K      = 60         (unused when disabled)
    pars.fit.hsvdClean.range  = [8.75 12]  (used only if re-enabled)


4. BASELINE SPLINE (pars.fit.baselineOpt)
-----------------------------------------
Problem:  Unknown whether the default amplitude L2 penalty on spline
          coefficients (lambdaAmpl=0.1) was helping or hurting.

Swept:    enable {on, off}, knotSpacing in {0.25, 0.5, 1, 2} (on a
          1 ppm fit range [8.7 9.7]), lambda in {0, 1, 10, 100},
          lambdaAmpl in {0, 0.1, 1}. 15 conditions (coarse + follow-up).

Winner:   lambdaAmpl=0 (other settings unchanged).
          Mean CV 5.14% -> 3.72%.
          - The entire improvement came from removing lambdaAmpl.
          - knotSpacing did NOT matter in [0.5, 2]: ks=0.5/1/2 all
            produced identical CV (3.72%) and per-group values when
            lambdaAmpl=0. On a 1 ppm fit range, all three yield only
            2-3 knots so the spline basis is effectively the same.
          - ks=0.25 overfits (5.85% at lambdaAmpl=0).
          - lambda=1 is well-chosen. lambda=0 worsens slightly,
            lambda>=10 is ruinous (>10%).
          - Disabling baseline entirely is much worse (7.6%) and
            gives ~10x higher residual RMS - the baseline is doing
            real work modeling broad signal.

Defaults set in processRepeatability.m (authoritative parseBaselineOpt
default in utils/curvefitAuto_varproBaseline.m was already 0):
    pars.fit.baselineOpt.enable      = true
    pars.fit.baselineOpt.knotSpacing = 2
    pars.fit.baselineOpt.lambda      = 1
    pars.fit.baselineOpt.lambdaAmpl  = 0
    pars.fit.baselineOpt.TolFun      = 1e-6

5. FIT PPM RANGE AND PEAK LIST (pars.fit.ppm_range, pars.peaks)
---------------------------------------------------------------
*** SUPERSEDED FOR BRAIN (processU01_Brain_1H.m): the "NAD+ 3 peaks only, no
*** Trp" conclusion held only because Trp was forced to share one fit region
*** and one lineshape kernel with NAD.  Brain now fits Trp in a SEPARATE
*** region ([9.8 10.45]) with its own baseline, which removes the ~1.4 pp NAD
*** penalty this section attributes to Trp.  See section 6.1. ***

Problem:  The original fit range [8.7 9.7] ppm was tight around the
          NAD+ H2/H6/H4 peaks. Unclear whether extending the window
          (more baseline support) or adding a Trp model peak (~10.1
          ppm) would help or hurt NAD+ repeatability.

Swept:    Coarse: upper edges 9.7 -> 11.0, lower edges 8.5 / 8.7,
          with/without Trp peak. 10 conditions.
          Fine: upper edges 9.75..10.05 step 0.05 (lower fixed 8.70);
          lower edges 8.60..8.90 step 0.05 (upper fixed 10.00).
          13 conditions.

Winner:   pars.fit.ppm_range = [8.70 10.00], NAD+ 3 peaks only.
          Mean CV 3.72% -> 3.12%.
          - Upper = 10.00 is a sharp local minimum. Moving +/-0.05
            loses 1.8-2 pp. 10.00 sits just below Trp, extending
            baseline support without pulling Trp signal in.
          - Lower = 8.70 is also the sweet spot. Lower (8.60-8.65)
            adds noise-only territory; higher (>=8.85) starts
            clipping H4 (center ~8.93, range 8.8-9.05).
          - Below 8.60 and at 8.90 the fit degrades sharply (~10%
            and ~22% respectively).
          - Adding a Trp peak costs ~1.4 pp in NAD+ CV by itself
            (parameter correlation with the shared asymmetric
            lineshape kernel and H4's tail). Trp inclusion fails
            the "minimal loss" threshold, so the default peak list
            stays NAD+ H2/H6/H4 only. If Trp is needed later, run
            a separate fit with its own tuned range.

Defaults set in processRepeatability.m:
    pars.fit.ppm_range = [8.7 10.0]
    pars.peaks.name    = {'NADH2','NADH6','NADH4'}
    pars.peaks.range   = [9.25 9.45; 9.05 9.25; 8.8 9.05]


OPTIMIZATION ORDER NOTE
-----------------------
Optimizations were applied sequentially; each sweep held previously-
optimized parameters fixed. The order matters: the lineshape sweep
exposed the FD-induced S139 regression (fixed by nSide=4), and the
HSVD sweep assumed the nSide=4 lineshape. No interaction effects
were re-checked at the end; a future full-factorial sweep could
confirm the optimum is global, but the large per-parameter margins
make this unlikely to change.

6. BRAIN DOWNFIELD FIT  (processU01_Brain_1H.m)
=============================================================================
Date:   2026-07-23
Scope:  12 DFS_CONTROL_STUDY control subjects (SXXX), 7T 1H downfield, 9.7 ppm
        excitation.  Peaks: NADH2 ~9.35, NADH6 ~9.17, NADH4 ~8.84, Trp indole
        NH ~10.1 ppm.
Driver: processU01_Brain_1H.m.  Full per-parameter rationale, sweep tables and
        rejected alternatives live in processU01_Brain_1H_NOTES.md; this is the
        summary.
Metric: (a) NAD SELF-CONSISTENCY -- H2/H6/H4 are single protons on one
            molecule, so after relaxation correction their concentrations must
            be equal; score = H6/H2 (and formerly H4/H2) vs 1.0.  This has a
            physics-defined answer and is the primary tuning signal.
        (b) between-subject CV of each peak.
        (c) agreement with SpecTickle (time-domain HSVD).  CORROBORATION ONLY
            -- there is NO gold standard for NAD quant, so SpecTickle agreement
            is never used as an accuracy target, and section 6.1/6.5 show a
            case where per-subject agreement is actively misleading.

WHAT CHANGED FROM THE APRIL PIPELINE (this driver vs processRepeatability.m):
  - Trp is now fit, in its own region             (6.1; overturns section 5)
  - lineshape kernel OFF                           (6.2; overturns section 2)
  - coarse VARPRO replaced by HSVD seeding         (6.3; new)
  - soft HSVD width prior added                    (6.4; new)
  - per-region baseline DOF set from data          (6.5; refines section 4)
  - areasConv made phase-invariant                 (6.6; new, SETTLED)


6.1  TWO-REGION FIT (Trp split out from NAD)
--------------------------------------------
Determination: fit two independent regions, each with its own coarse ->
    phase -> fine pass (coarseMode 'perRegion'):
        region 1 "NAD" : [8.7 10.0],  peaks NADH2/NADH6/NADH4
        region 2 "Trp" : [9.8 10.45], peak  Trp
Why:  a SINGLE wide region cannot serve both -- a baseline stiff enough to not
      nibble the broad NADH4 peak inflates Trp ~2x, and one flexible enough for
      Trp eats NADH4.  Splitting gives each its own baseline stiffness.  It also
      keeps low-SNR Trp (longest lever arm) out of the NAD phase ramp, and gives
      Trp a pure zeroth-order phase (single peak => pc1=0), which stopped Trp
      railing at the +/-60 deg ph_range bound in 4/12 subjects.
      This is why section 5's "adding Trp costs 1.4 pp of NAD CV" no longer
      applies: that cost was Trp sharing NAD's region and kernel.
Retired single-region alt ([8.7 10.3], all 4 peaks): H4/H2 = 2.44 vs 1.083
      expected, Trp/H2 CV 45%.  Kept commented in the driver for A/B.
Region-1 lower edge 8.7 not 8.6: the extra 0.1 ppm turns up into an unmodelled
      ~8.5 ppm ATP2/adenine peak the stiff spline cannot follow, dumping misfit
      onto NADH4.  Known cost: ~15% of NADH4's Lorentzian mass sits outside the
      window (NADH4 is deprioritized -- may not be reported).


6.2  LINESHAPE KERNEL -> OFF  (overturns section 2)
---------------------------------------------------
Determination: pars.fit.lineShapeOpt.enable = false.
Why:  the shared kernel and the Voigt Gaussian width are collinear (both model
      non-Lorentzian distortion), and the kernel was winning -- widthG sat
      railed at its LOWER bound in 11/12 NADH2 fits and 8/12 NADH6 fits, i.e.
      the Voigt's Gaussian term contributed nothing.  Large 7T voxels should
      carry genuine Gaussian B0 character, so the Voigt is the physical model;
      turning the kernel off lets widthG float.
Section 2 (enable=true, nSide=4) was tuned on a DIFFERENT metric (scan-rescan
      repeatability, not NAD self-consistency) and driver; it stands there.
OPEN:  widthG STILL rails with the kernel off (18/36 NAD fits) => mode 5 (Voigt)
      should drop to a pure LORENTZIAN.  Not yet actioned.
      Kernel settings kept (disabled) in the driver for A/B tests.


6.3  HSVD COARSE SEEDING  (new)
-------------------------------
Determination: replace the coarse VARPRO with an HSVD decomposition that seeds
      peak position / width / phase and the first-order phase correction.
      K=60, npts=1024 (SpecTickle defaults); accept windows from SpecTickle's
      7T peak table (NAD 10-90 Hz, Trp 10-120 Hz); phaseTolDeg=100.
Why:  the old coarse phase estimate was broken -- an unweighted polyfit through
      4 peaks, dominated by low-SNR Trp at the longest lever arm, leaving a
      ~74 deg/ppm residual ramp.  HSVD seeding + the perRegion split (6.1) cut
      the worst-case residual phase across the 12 subjects from 90 to 34 deg.
phaseTolDeg MATTERS: at the archived +/-180 (criterion off), our wider peak
      windows admit inverted components -- e.g. S095 H6 seeded at -178 deg
      (inverted) and wrecked the ramp; at +/-100 it lands at +37 deg.


6.4  HSVD SOFT PRIORS  (new)
----------------------------
Determination: feed the HSVD linewidths back into the fine fit as a soft
      PENALTY, not a hard cap:
        pars.fit.baselineOpt.lambdaWidth = 0.3   (Voigt FWHM vs HSVD lw)
        pars.fit.baselineOpt.lambdaPrior = 0.0   (baseline prior OFF)
Why soft: a hard width cap pins the width on its bound and dumps the
      discrepancy into the AMPLITUDE (the quantity of interest).  The 45 Hz Trp
      cap tried first moved Trp from 2.9x SpecTickle to 3x BELOW it.
Sweep (4x4 lambdaPrior x lambdaWidth, all 12, scored on mean|NAD ratio-1|):
      - the WIDTH prior does nearly all the work and SATURATES: 0 -> 0.05 halves
        mean|dev| (0.470 -> 0.209); 0.05..2.0 is flat.  0.3 sits mid-plateau.
      - the BASELINE prior is HARMFUL and is OFF: HSVD's lw_max truncates broad
        wings, so real peak signal sits in its "baseline"; pulling the spline
        toward it hands peak area away (NADH4 CV -> 63-90%).  Hook kept for a
        better baseline estimate (untruncated HSVD, or a measured MM spectrum).


6.5  PER-REGION BASELINE DOF  (refines section 4)
-------------------------------------------------
Mechanics: knotSpacing is a SENTINEL, not a ppm spacing -- makeSplineBasis uses
      nIntervals = max(2, round(width/knotSpacing)), and coef = nIntervals + 3.
      knotSpacing=2 always floors to 2 intervals = 5 coefficients.

Region 1 (NAD): knotSpacing 0.43 -> 3 intervals -> 6 COEFFICIENTS.
      Set from the median flexibility SpecTickle's own unassigned HSVD
      components need over [8.7 10.0] (analyze_spectickle_baseline.py).

Region 2 (Trp): sentinel 2 -> 5 COEFFICIENTS.
      A 6-coef variant (knotSpacing 0.22) was TRIED AND REJECTED:
        - it wins on Trp-window residual (0.0094 vs 0.0127), BUT
        - it puts an interior local MINIMUM in the baseline in 12/12 subjects,
          ~0.10 ppm from the fitted Trp centre, ~18% of Trp peak height deep
          (check_baseline_monotonicity.py).
      That dip is physically inadmissible: inside 9.8-10.45 every baseline
      contributor is centred BELOW the window, so a sum of absorption-mode
      Lorentzian tails must be MONOTONE DECREASING.  The notch is the spline
      digging under Trp so Trp can be larger -- peak/baseline degeneracy.  At
      5 coef there is no interior minimum in any subject.
      LESSON: a residual gain bought by an inadmissible baseline shape is
      degeneracy absorbing mismatch, not model improvement; a physical
      constraint violated 12/12 outranks a small residual gain.
      DO NOT re-select region-2 DOF on Trp-window residual, and DO NOT re-select
      on SpecTickle agreement: per-subject Trp:NADH2 correlates NEGATIVELY
      between the pipelines (r = -0.79), so mean-level agreement is coincidental
      (and swings on the single subject S095).


6.6  PHASE-INVARIANT areasConv  (new -- SETTLED, do not reconsider)
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Determination: quantitation = magnitude of the complex amplitude, taken as the
      frequency-domain area of the ZERO-PHASE model peak (kernel/Voigt shape
      retained, per-peak phase block zeroed before integration).  Implemented in
      utils/curvefitAuto_varproBaseline.m; consumed as output.met.fit.areasConv.
Why:  the reported quantity must not depend on the fitted phase.  The old path
      integrated real(component) with its fitted phase, scaling area by
      cos(phase) -- a real bug that under-counted peaks carrying residual phase.
Effect: Trp 0.196 -> 0.221 mM, NAD H6/H2 SD 0.105 -> 0.081, mean|dev|
      0.201 -> 0.185.

OPEN ISSUES
-----------
  - widthG rails with the kernel off => mode 5 should become pure Lorentzian.
  - Trp T1/T2 are placeholders.
  - Per-subject concentrations correlate poorly with SpecTickle (NADH2 r=0.016;
    Trp:NADH2 r=-0.79) -- at most one pipeline tracks real biology.  No age
    effect survives in either (n=12, only 2 subjects >40 y).
  - S167 is a clear failure (H6/H2=5.33, region-1 pc1 unwrapping-branch flip);
    small phase residuals do NOT prove the ramp is right.
  - Going below the 8.6 ppm region-1 edge needs an explicit ~8.52 ppm nuisance
    peak first.
